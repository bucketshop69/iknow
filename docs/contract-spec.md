# iknow Contract Specification

This document specifies the contract behavior for the iknow MVP: binary, USDC-collateralized prediction markets with AMM trading, LP participation, and deterministic resolution. It is intentionally contract-facing and should be treated as the reference for implementing and testing issues #1 and #5.

## Scope

The MVP supports:

- Binary markets with exactly two outcomes: `YES` and `NO`.
- USDC as the only collateral asset.
- Complete-set minting and merging.
- Creator-seeded AMM liquidity.
- LP add/remove liquidity flows.
- Trading before market close.
- Resolution to `YES`, `NO`, or `INVALID`.
- Redemption after final resolution.

The MVP does not support:

- Multi-outcome markets.
- Leveraged positions or undercollateralized borrowing.
- Order book matching.
- Autonomous AI-only resolution.
- Cross-chain collateral inside market contracts.

## Actors

- `creator`: creates the market, posts any required bond, and may seed initial liquidity.
- `trader`: buys, sells, mints, merges, and redeems outcome exposure.
- `lp`: adds or removes AMM liquidity and earns trading fees while taking inventory risk.
- `resolver`: authorized address or contract that finalizes the market outcome.
- `feeRecipient`: address that receives protocol or creator fees, if enabled.

The same address may play multiple roles unless a specific implementation restricts that role.

## Collateral And Units

Markets are collateralized in USDC with 6 decimals. All contract accounting denominates collateral amounts in smallest USDC units.

```txt
1 USDC = 1_000_000 units
```

Implementations must not assume 18-decimal collateral for pricing, fee, or redemption math.

## Complete-Set Solvency

The core invariant is:

```txt
1 USDC locked -> 1 YES + 1 NO minted
1 YES + 1 NO burned -> 1 USDC unlocked
```

Complete-set solvency requirements:

- Outcome tokens may only be minted as matched `YES` and `NO` sets against USDC transferred into the market.
- A complete set may be merged only by burning equal amounts of `YES` and `NO`, returning the same amount of USDC collateral.
- The market must never mint unmatched winning-token redemption claims.
- Before resolution, the market's redemption liability equals the number of outstanding complete sets after accounting for merged sets and AMM inventory.
- After resolution, each outstanding winning token redeems for 1 USDC unit per token unit, and losing tokens redeem for 0.
- Protocol and trading fees must be funded from trade proceeds or explicit fee flows, not by reducing collateral reserved for outstanding complete-set redemption unless the user-facing trade math already accounts for that fee.
- Rounding must favor solvency. Any dust generated by AMM math, fee math, or fixed-point division must remain in the market or be handled through an explicit dust policy that cannot make redemptions insolvent.

A useful accounting check for tests:

```txt
USDC held by market >= redeemable winning outcome token supply
```

For unresolved markets, tests should track complete-set backing rather than final winner liability:

```txt
USDC held by market >= min(total YES supply, total NO supply)
```

Implementations may hold additional USDC for fees, unclaimed LP proceeds, bonds, or dust.

## Market Lifecycle

### 1. Drafting

Market drafting happens off-chain or in frontend/API tooling. A valid market spec should include:

- Question.
- Outcome definitions for `YES` and `NO`.
- Resolution source.
- Close time.
- Resolution time or resolution window.
- Invalid conditions.
- Creator address.
- Initial liquidity amount, if any.
- Fee settings.

AI tooling may help produce the market spec and evidence packet, but AI output is advisory only.

### 2. Creation

Market creation initializes immutable or governance-controlled market parameters:

- Collateral token address must be USDC-compatible and 6-decimal.
- Close time must be in the future.
- Resolver must be nonzero.
- Fee settings must be within configured bounds.
- Initial liquidity must satisfy the implementation's minimum, if one exists.
- Creator bond and initial liquidity must be accounted separately, even when transferred in the same transaction.

Current MVP policy sets a 5 USDC minimum creator bond and a 10 USDC minimum initial liquidity amount. This is enforced in the web app, API draft/create flow, and factory contract source. Existing local or testnet deployments must be redeployed before their on-chain bytecode reflects the same rule.

If initial liquidity is provided, the market mints complete sets and deposits balanced inventory into the AMM according to the initial price assumptions.

### 3. Open Trading

While the market is open:

- Traders may buy or sell `YES` and `NO` through the AMM.
- Users may mint complete sets by depositing USDC.
- Users may merge complete sets to withdraw USDC, unless the implementation intentionally restricts merging while liquidity is locked.
- LPs may add liquidity using USDC-backed complete sets or an equivalent implementation-specific flow.
- LPs may remove liquidity subject to pool accounting and any minimum-liquidity rules.

Trading must stop once the market reaches close time or enters a paused/closed state.

### 4. Closed, Awaiting Resolution

After close:

- New directional trades must be disabled.
- Resolution evidence may be posted off-chain, indexed, or emitted by an event depending on implementation.
- The resolver may finalize `YES`, `NO`, or `INVALID` according to the market spec.
- LP removal before resolution is implementation-specific, but must preserve complete-set solvency.

### 5. Finalized

Once finalized:

- Outcome cannot change except through a deliberately specified dispute or emergency process.
- Winning outcome holders may redeem.
- Losing outcome holders receive no collateral.
- Invalid-market redemption follows the invalid policy.
- LP claims settle according to their share of remaining pool assets and the finalized outcome.

Implementations must guard against double redemption, double LP withdrawal, and reentrancy during all USDC transfers.

## Fee Boundaries

Fees are part of the MVP trade model and must be bounded.

MVP fee split:

- `TOTAL_FEE_BPS`: 30 bps (0.30%).
- `LP_FEE_BPS`: 20 bps (0.20%).
- `CREATOR_FEE_BPS`: 5 bps (0.05%).
- `PROTOCOL_FEE_BPS`: 5 bps (0.05%).
- `BPS_DENOMINATOR`: 10_000.

Fee requirements:

- Fee parameters must be validated at market creation and on any admin update.
- Total fees charged to a trade must not exceed the configured total cap.
- Fees must be transparent in quoted input/output amounts.
- Fee rounding must not cause the user to receive less than the stated minimum output or pay more than the stated maximum input.
- Fee collection must not break the complete-set solvency invariant.
- Fee recipients must be nonzero when a nonzero fee is configured.
- LP fees are LP-owned and paid according to per-share accrual when LP shares are removed.
- LP fees accrue to shares that exist when the fee is earned; LPs who join later cannot capture previously accrued fees.
- Creator fees are claimable only after a non-`INVALID` resolution.
- Protocol fees are claimable by the configured protocol fee recipient.
- If a market resolves `INVALID`, unclaimed creator fees are forfeited into the LP fee bucket.

## LP Assumptions

LPs are market makers, not guaranteed-yield depositors.

LP assumptions:

- LPs earn trading fees if the implementation routes fees to liquidity.
- LPs take inventory risk as traders move the pool price.
- LPs take adverse-selection risk near resolution.
- LP shares represent a claim on the pool's assets, not a fixed USDC balance.
- LP fee claims must be tracked by share ownership at accrual time, not by only reading the current share supply at withdrawal time.
- LP withdrawals must burn or account for LP shares before transferring assets.
- Initial liquidity may be permanently locked in a small minimum amount to avoid first-LP share inflation attacks.
- Removing liquidity must not let an LP withdraw collateral needed to back outstanding complete sets held outside the pool.

The AMM design may be constant-product, LMSR-like, or custom, but tests should assert the protocol-level invariant rather than a specific curve unless the implementation chooses one.

## Resolution Outcomes

Resolution is finalization of exactly one enum-like state:

- `YES`: `YES` tokens redeem 1 USDC per unit; `NO` tokens redeem 0.
- `NO`: `NO` tokens redeem 1 USDC per unit; `YES` tokens redeem 0.
- `INVALID`: market cannot be fairly resolved under its spec.

Invalid policy should be explicit before deployment. For the MVP, the preferred invalid behavior is complete-set unwind where possible:

- Holders of matched `YES` and `NO` can merge complete sets for USDC.
- If single-sided invalid redemption is supported, each `YES` and each `NO` redeems for 0.5 USDC, with rounding down and residual dust retained by the market or sent through an explicit dust policy.
- Creator bonds are refunded after non-`INVALID` resolution and slashed into the protocol fee bucket on `INVALID`.

The implementation must choose one invalid policy and test it directly. It must not leave invalid redemption ambiguous.

Resolution requirements:

- Only the authorized resolver or resolution module may finalize.
- Market must be closed before finalization unless an emergency cancellation path is explicitly specified.
- Finalization must emit an event with market address/id, outcome, resolver, and evidence URI or evidence hash if available.
- Redemption must be unavailable before finalization.
- Redemption must burn or mark claimed tokens before transferring USDC.

## Test Harness Expectations

Shared Foundry tests should use:

- A 6-decimal mock USDC.
- Stable actor addresses for `creator`, `trader`, `lp`, `resolver`, `feeRecipient`, and `treasury`.
- Constants for `ONE_USDC`, fee basis points, and representative liquidity/trade amounts.
- Helpers that mint USDC to actors and approve spenders.

Production contracts are out of scope for this spec file; tests should import the shared base and layer implementation-specific assertions in separate test files.
